BD Radar: the dashboard

The idea in one line: every morning each person opens it and sees five named people to contact, why now, our way in, and what to say. Nobody gets told to "do BD".

What every row must have

No name, no row. A row is not ready until it has all of these:

FieldExample
CompanyA mid size Brisbane multi res builder
Trigger (why now)DA approved for a 12 storey tower in Woolloongabba, 6 Nov
Decision makerNamed Construction Manager or MD, with LinkedIn link and mobile

OpenerOne line they can say on the call, written for that person

Where the triggers come from

1. Development approvals (earliest signal)
  • Brisbane City Council Development.i, plus Gold Coast, Logan, Moreton Bay and Sunshine Coast portals
  • Filter: approved, multi res, commercial, mixed use, above a set value
  • Action: find the developer, find who is likely to build it, call before the tender
2. Tenders and awards
  • EstimateOne (shows which builders are pricing a job and who wins)
  • QTenders and ICN Gateway for government work, Cordell if you want to pay for it
  • Action: a builder that just won a job needs a site team in 4 to 8 weeks. Call the week it is awarded.
3. Hiring signals
  • Builders advertising on SEEK and LinkedIn for roles we fill (they have the pain right now)
  • Ads that have been up 3+ weeks or reposted = they are struggling
  • Action: call the hiring manager with a named candidate in hand
4. People moves and our network
  • Candidates we placed who are now senior (they become hiring managers)
  • Past clients quiet for 60+ days (from Atlas and the Xero placement list)
  • New CMs and GMs announced on LinkedIn
  • Action: congratulate, catch up, ask what is coming
5. Project news: The Urban Developer, Brisbane Development, Build Australia, the 2032 venue packages. Each story = a builder and a developer to call.

How it runs each week

  1. Monday 8:30 catch up: Claude refreshes the triggers and fills the Radar. You allocate 25 rows per person for the week (5 a day).
  2. Each morning: each person opens "My five today". Each card shows who, why now, our in, the opener and a call button.
  3. Each action gets an outcome logged in one tap. Anything marked "not now" gets a recall date and comes back automatically.
  4. Friday scoreboard: actions done, conversations had, meetings booked, briefs won. Shown per person, side by side.

The sourcing skill: tips for Heidi

Why the lists have been weak so far: they match job titles, not careers. These are the rules from every list you have marked up, built in from day one.

The rules the skill must enforce

  1. Start from the client's competitors, not keywords. List the 15 to 20 builders who do the same work at the same size, then pull their current staff. This alone fixes most of the bad lists.
  2. Passive first. Order: your LinkedIn 1st degree, people you have messaged, then competitor staff. SEEK applicants last.
  3. Current title, right level. Foreman roles get foremen and leading hands only, not supervisors or SMs. PM roles exclude CMs, GMs, directors and owners.
  4. Verify on LinkedIn before listing. Confirm the current employer (SEEK data goes stale), confirm they have actually built, confirm tenure.
  5. Google every company. Out: civil and infrastructure (for building roles), subbies, volume home builders, insurance builders, tiny reno outfits.
  6. Brisbane only unless the brief says otherwise.
  7. Hard exclusions: anyone who worked at the client, anyone already messaged for this role, the do not touch list (for example the MCD Group list).
  8. Write the reason. Every name gets one line on why they fit. If there is a red flag they go on a "ruled out" tab, never "check".
  9. Fewer, better. 15 verified names beat 80 guesses.
  10. LinkedIn safety. Office hours, small batches, about 15 connects a day, one LinkedIn job at a time across the whole team. Any warning = stop.

How to build it with Heidi

  1. Write the scorecard first. Take one live role (the Arlo Senior SM is perfect) and agree what a 10/10 candidate looks like: builder type, project size, years, current title, location, must haves.
  2. Build the "brief intake" step. The skill asks 6 questions before it searches: role, client, project type and value, salary band, must haves, who is off limits.
  3. Build the competitor map step. The skill lists similar builders and you approve the list before any searching.
  4. Test it on a role you already filled. Run it for the Melrose PM brief and see if Grant and Deen come up. If they do not, the rules are wrong.
  5. Mark up the output. Heidi ticks or crosses each name with a reason. Every cross becomes a new rule in the skill.
  6. Fix the output format. One spreadsheet: Warm tab (message first), Competitor tab (connect), Ruled out tab. Each row: name, current title, company, years, LinkedIn link, why they fit.
  7. Package it. Once it passes on two roles, I turn it into a skill file that everyone pastes into their own Claude. Same rules for all three of you.
